01

Overview

HIVE is a proximity-tracking feature for groups of BLE tags. You select a set of 2 to 5 tags that belong together — a travel kit, a tool collection, a delivery load — and HIVE watches their positions. If any tag drifts further than your configured distance from the others, you get an alert.

HIVE works with any iBeacon or Eddystone BLE tags already registered in Pinplot. It uses the same crowdsourced location data your tags already report, no special hardware or anchor required. Tags report their position whenever a phone running a compatible app passes nearby, which means data quality is best in busy areas and naturally sparser in quiet ones.

As of v1.1, HIVE is motion-aware. When your hive is travelling together (in a car, on a bike, in your bag while walking), HIVE knows the difference between "really separated" and "just on the move," and adjusts its behaviour to avoid false alerts.

→ At a Glance

Group 2–5 tags per hive · Configurable distance threshold · Per-hive mobility profile · 60-day operational lifetime · 60-day archive retention · Permanent delete option · Motion-aware evaluation (new).

02

What's New in v1.1

New

v1.1 is a substantial update focused on a single problem: HIVE wasn't handling moving hives well. If you've ever had your travel kit fire a "breach" alert while you were just driving home from the supermarket, this release is for you.

The Problem

BLE tag positions are reported by passing phones — typically every 2–5 minutes. When your hive is sitting still, this is fine: a position from 3 minutes ago is still roughly correct. But when your hive is moving in a vehicle at 40 km/h, a 3-minute-old position is over 2 km away from where the tag actually is now.

The old version of HIVE didn't account for this. It would compare a tag's fresh position against another tag's older position, see them as 1+ km apart, and fire a breach alert — even though both tags were sitting peacefully next to each other in the same boot of the same car.

The Solution

HIVE now detects when your hive is in motion and pauses proximity checks until you stop. When you arrive somewhere and settle, normal monitoring resumes automatically. You see a clear "In Transit" indicator on your hive while this is happening, so you always know what HIVE is doing.

What You Need to Do

Nothing. Motion awareness is automatic and driven by your hive's existing mobility profile. There are no new settings to configure. Existing hives benefit from the new behaviour immediately on upgrade.

→ One Important Trade-off

While your hive is In Transit, proximity alerts are paused. If a tag is genuinely lost mid-journey, you'll find out when you stop and HIVE re-evaluates. For most users (whose tagged items are with them) this is the right behaviour — you can't usefully act on an alert while driving anyway.

03

How It Works

Every time one of your tagged items reports a new position, HIVE re-evaluates the hive. It calculates the distance between every pair of tags in the group and finds the largest distance — the group diameter. If the diameter exceeds your threshold and the hive isn't in motion, an alert fires.

The Alert State Machine

Alerts don't fire on the first sign of trouble — that would create noise from GPS jitter and brief edge cases. Instead, hives move through a deliberate sequence of states:

OK
Figure 1. Alert state machine — confirmation required to enter breach, hysteresis required to clear back to OK.

A suspected breach moves the hive to PENDING while waiting for a confirming report. Once confirmed, it transitions to BREACHING and the alert is sent. When the wandering tag rejoins, the hive doesn't immediately clear — it must drop comfortably below the threshold before transitioning through RESOLVING back to OK.

Outlier Detection

When a breach fires, HIVE identifies which tag caused it by counting how many over-threshold pairs each tag participates in. The tag with the most breaching pairs is flagged as the outlier — usually the one that wandered off — and is highlighted in alerts and the detail view.

Motion-Aware Evaluation (v1.1)

The state machine above runs only when your hive is Stationary. When the hive is detected as In Transit or Settling, proximity evaluation is paused. See Motion States for full details.

04

Quick Start

Getting your first hive running takes under a minute if you accept the suggested settings.

  1. From the sidebar, choose HIVE Tracking, then click + New Hive.
  2. Pick the tags you want to group (between 2 and 5). Tags already in another hive will be unavailable.
  3. Give the hive a name — something descriptive like Travel Kit or Workshop Tools.
  4. HIVE analyses your tags' recent movement and suggests a mobility profile and distance threshold. If the badge says High Confidence, click Use Suggested Settings and you're done.
  5. Otherwise, click Review Settings to walk through the 3-question wizard.

Your hive begins tracking immediately. There's a short 5–10 minute settling period where alerts are suppressed while HIVE establishes baseline state. After that, alerts fire normally — except when your hive is moving.

→ Tip

If you've used HIVE before, the wizard remembers your last configuration. Recreating a hive with similar settings takes just a few clicks.

05

Key Concepts

Group Diameter

The largest distance between any two tags in your hive. If your hive has 5 tags, HIVE measures all 10 pairwise distances and reports the biggest one. The group is "together" when the diameter stays below your threshold.

Distance Threshold

The maximum group diameter you'll tolerate before an alert fires. Configurable per hive, and shown alongside the live diameter on every screen. Common values range from 50m (very close) to 1km (roughly together).

Mobility Profile

A bundled preset that controls how HIVE interprets your tags' position data. Affects freshness windows, alert sensitivity, motion detection thresholds, and confirmation requirements. Three options: Stationary, Slow, and Mobile.

Freshness Tier

Each tag is classified by how recently it reported. Tags fade through tiers as they go silent: FRESH RECENT STALE LOST. Only Fresh and Recent tags participate in alert evaluation.

Motion State NEW v1.1

A separate state from the alert state, indicating whether your hive is at rest or moving. Four values: STATIONARY STARTING IN TRANSIT STOPPING. Only Stationary hives are evaluated for proximity breaches. See Motion States.

Hive Lifecycle

Every hive has a 60-day operational life from the moment it's created. After that, it moves to a 60-day archive where you can review its history but it no longer tracks. After the archive period, data is purged unless you act.

06

Setup Wizard

The wizard is a 3-question flow designed to take seconds, not minutes. It pre-fills every answer based on your tags' recent activity, so most users can simply confirm and continue.

Question 1 — What are you tracking?

A descriptive name for the hive. Optional but recommended — alerts and history reference this name, so something concrete like Travel Kit beats Hive 4.

Question 2 — How much do these items move?

The mobility profile choice. HIVE pre-selects this based on how your tags have moved in the last few weeks, marked with a SUGGESTED badge. Three options to pick from:

  • They stay put — items that shouldn't normally move from where you've placed them. Tools in a workshop, equipment in a yard, instruments in a lab.
  • They move occasionally — items that move sometimes, slowly. Livestock, parked vehicles, mobility aids, luggage during a trip. HIVE expects extended periods of rest with occasional movement.
  • They're often on the move — items carried with you day-to-day or in transit. Personal belongings, delivery packages, fleet equipment. HIVE monitors them when at rest; in-transit periods are paused and resume at the destination.

See Mobility Profiles for full details.

Question 3 — How close should they stay?

The distance threshold. Choose from preset values (Very Close 50m, Close 100m, Nearby 250m, In the Same Area 500m, Roughly Together 1km) or enter a custom value. The default matches your selected mobility profile.

→ Express Path

If HIVE has high confidence in its suggestions (typically when your tags have at least 7 days of history), an express card appears at the top of the wizard. Click Use Suggested Settings to skip the questions entirely.

07

Mobility Profiles

Mobility profiles are the most important configuration choice. They tell HIVE what kind of asset you're tracking, which affects almost every aspect of how alerts are evaluated — including motion detection behaviour.

ProfileBest ForDefault ThresholdMotion Detection
Stationary Tools, equipment, instruments — assets that shouldn't normally move 150m Disabled (any motion is alerted)
Slow Livestock, parked vehicles, mobility aids, luggage during a trip 450m Active above 10 km/h
Mobile Personal belongings, shared equipment, couriered packages, fleet items in transit 1000m Active above 15 km/h

What Profiles Actually Change

Picking a profile sets several parameters at once. As mobility increases, freshness windows shorten (older positions become less trustworthy faster), confirmation requirements tighten, noise filters widen, and motion detection becomes more aggressive.

The Mobile profile covers a wide range — from personal items that mostly sit still to delivery packages in active transit. HIVE is most accurate when these items are at rest; alerts during transit are paused and resume when the hive arrives at its destination. See In Transit Behaviour for full details.

The Default Threshold column shows the starting value for each profile, calibrated for typical 5–10 minute reporting cadence. You can adjust the threshold up or down for your specific use case during setup or later in hive settings.

→ Picking the Wrong Profile

If your hive consistently produces too many false alerts or misses real ones, the profile is probably mismatched. HIVE will surface a gentle suggestion if it detects this. You can change the profile mid-life — see Delete & Recreate.

Detail View

Clicking any hive card opens its detail view — the deepest workspace for understanding hive state and history.

Live Position Map

Shows each tag's most recent position relative to the others. The dashed circle marks your distance threshold. Connecting lines show pairwise distances — green lines for in-bounds pairs, dashed red lines for over-threshold pairs leading to the outlier.

Diameter Readout

A live numeric display of the current group diameter, shown alongside a horizontal bar chart with the threshold marker. When the bar crosses the marker and the hive is Stationary, you're in breach. When the hive is In Transit, the diameter is shown but annotated as "(in transit — not evaluated)" so you know it isn't being judged for breaches.

Tag Table

A row for each tag showing ID, name, last-seen time, distance from group centre, and role (core or outlier). Sortable on any column. Stale or lost tags are dimmed.

Side Panels

The right column groups several panels:

  • Motion State NEW — Current motion state (Stationary / In Transit / Settling), with a small visual indicator and time-in-state
  • Configuration — Current mobility profile, threshold, freshness windows, and other parameters
  • Lifecycle — Days remaining on the 60-day clock, with a quick Recreate Hive button
  • Recent Activity — Timeline of evaluations, motion state changes, and alerts over the last 24 hours

Alerts & Breaches

HIVE issues different alert types depending on data quality, motion state, and what changed. Knowing the difference helps you respond appropriately.

Alert TypeMeaningAction
Confirmed Breach Multiple fresh reports show the hive is split (Stationary only) Verify and act — most reliable signal
Suspected Breach One fresh report shows a breach, awaiting confirmation Watch for escalation; may auto-resolve
Lost Contact A tag has gone silent beyond the lost threshold Check tag battery and physical condition
Partially Observable Too many tags stale to evaluate confidently Often resolves on its own as data flows in
Breach Cleared The hive returned to OK state Informational — confirms the issue is resolved
Resolved by Transit NEW An active breach was resolved because the hive started moving Informational — monitoring resumes when you stop

Why Alerts May Take Time

Because tag positions arrive from crowdsourced sources, alert latency depends on how often phones pass nearby. In urban areas, breaches typically confirm within 3–8 minutes. In rural or low-traffic areas, it can take 15–30 minutes or longer. This isn't a system delay — it's the time required to gather enough evidence to be confident.

→ Honest About Uncertainty

HIVE will surface "data quality degraded" or "in transit" rather than fire low-confidence alerts. If you see one of these, it means the hive can't confidently say what's happening — usually because tags have gone quiet or the hive is moving, not because something's wrong.

12

Motion Awareness

New

The biggest change in v1.1: HIVE now recognises when your hive is on the move and adjusts its behaviour accordingly. This eliminates a category of false-positive breach alerts that used to fire whenever a hive travelled together in a vehicle.

The Underlying Problem

BLE tags don't have GPS. Their positions come from passing phones, which means each tag in your hive gets an updated position roughly every 2–5 minutes — but at different times for different tags. When everyone's standing still, this is fine. When everyone's in a moving car, two tags reported a few minutes apart appear to be far apart, even though they're sitting next to each other.

At urban driving speeds (around 30 km/h), a 2-minute-old position is up to 1 km behind reality. Without motion awareness, this looks indistinguishable from a real separation.

The Solution

HIVE now tracks the centre of your hive over time. If the centre is moving consistently in a direction at vehicle speed, and the individual tags' movements agree with that direction, HIVE concludes the hive is travelling together and pauses proximity checks until you stop.

What You'll See

When your hive enters motion, the status changes to IN TRANSIT on the hive list and detail views. A friendly indicator explains:

→ In Transit

Your hive is on the move — proximity monitoring paused. Will resume monitoring when stopped for a few minutes.

What Happens to Existing Alerts

If a breach alert was active when the hive started moving, it's automatically resolved with a "Resolved by Transit" notification. This isn't HIVE saying the breach was wrong — it's HIVE acknowledging that the data is no longer reliable enough to maintain the alert. When you stop, the hive is re-evaluated fresh.

What Stays Active

Even during transit, HIVE continues:

  • Showing live tag positions on the map
  • Tracking tag freshness and lost contact (a tag battery dying is meaningful regardless of motion)
  • Logging position history for later review

Motion States

New

Every hive has a motion state in addition to its alert state. The motion state determines how (or whether) HIVE evaluates proximity.

● Stationary
At Rest
Centre of the hive isn't moving. Normal proximity evaluation runs. All alert types active.
● Starting
Just Started
Initial motion detected. Brief pause (~30s–2min) while HIVE confirms the hive is really moving. Alerts paused.
● In Transit
On the Move
Centre is moving consistently with coherent group motion. Proximity alerts paused. Tags still tracked.
● Stopping
Just Stopped
Recently came to rest. Brief pause (3–5 min) while position data stabilises. Then re-evaluates.

How HIVE Detects Motion

HIVE computes the geometric centre of all your tags' fresh positions, and tracks how it moves over time. The journey through motion has two transitional buffer states (Starting and Stopping) on either side of full In Transit, so that proximity alerts are paused across the whole motion lifecycle — not just the middle.

To enter In Transit, two conditions must both be true:

  1. The centre has been moving faster than the profile's threshold (15 km/h for Mobile, 10 km/h for Slow) for at least 2 minutes
  2. Most individual tags' movements agree with the centre's direction (showing they're moving together as a group)

The second condition is important: it prevents one drifting tag from making the system think the whole hive is moving.

Figure 2. Motion state machine — four states form a cycle, with Starting and Stopping as buffer states either side of In Transit.
  • Stationary → Starting: centre starts moving faster than threshold for ~30 seconds
  • Starting → In Transit: motion sustained for 2 minutes total with coherent group motion
  • Starting → Stationary: motion didn't last (e.g. brief burst that stopped) — alerts resume
  • In Transit → Stopping: centre stable (slower than threshold) for 3 minutes
  • Stopping → Stationary: continued stability for ~5 minutes total post-stop, then full re-evaluation
  • Stopping → In Transit: motion re-detected (handles brief stops at traffic lights)

Why Two Buffer States?

Position data is unreliable both at the start of motion (we need to confirm it's real) and at the end of motion (the trailing edge of in-motion reports keeps arriving for a few minutes). Starting and Stopping are symmetric — both pause alert evaluation during transitional moments where the data can't be trusted. This eliminates false breach alerts across the whole journey, not just the middle.

14

In Transit Behaviour

New

This section walks through what HIVE does as your hive moves through the full motion lifecycle — from the first hint of motion, through full transit, to settled at destination.

What's Suppressed (and When)

Proximity alerts are suppressed across the entire motion lifecycle — Starting, In Transit, and Stopping. You'll only get proximity alerts when your hive is fully Stationary.

  • Confirmed Breach alerts — paused throughout Starting / In Transit / Stopping
  • Suspected Breach alerts — paused throughout Starting / In Transit / Stopping
  • Active alerts at the moment motion starts — automatically resolved with "Resolved by Transit" notification

What Stays Active

  • Live position display — you can still see where your tags are on the map
  • Lost Contact alerts — if a tag goes completely silent for hours, you still want to know (battery dead, tag damaged)
  • Position history logging — every report is still recorded for later review
  • Motion state changes — recorded in the activity timeline

What Happens When You Start Moving

When the hive's centre starts moving above threshold, the motion state transitions to STARTING. Alerts are suppressed immediately. HIVE then waits up to 2 minutes to confirm the motion is real and sustained.

  • If motion is sustained: the hive transitions to IN TRANSIT
  • If motion stops before 2 minutes: the hive returns to STATIONARY and runs a fresh proximity evaluation. Any genuine breach that happened during the brief motion is then captured.

What Happens When You Stop

When the hive's centre stops moving, the motion state transitions to STOPPING. During Stopping, evaluation remains paused for 3–5 minutes (depending on profile). This is to avoid noise from the trailing edge of stale-but-arriving reports.

Once Stopping completes, the hive transitions to STATIONARY and runs a fresh proximity evaluation:

  • If everything's together: nothing happens (no alert needed). The hive is back to normal.
  • If a tag is genuinely missing: a Confirmed Breach alert fires with copy that acknowledges the timing. Example: "Travel Kit arrived but Headphones (T-04) is missing. Last seen 35 minutes ago, 12 km from current position."

Brief Stops Don't Restart the Cycle

Stopping at traffic lights for a couple of minutes doesn't transition the hive into Stopping. The 10-minute settling requirement means brief urban stops are absorbed naturally — the hive remains In Transit until you actually arrive somewhere.

Brief Starts Don't Lock You Out of Alerts

Similarly, a brief moment of motion that doesn't sustain (e.g. you picked up your bag, walked across the room, set it down) won't trap the hive in suppressed-alert mode. The Starting state has its own timeout — if motion doesn't sustain for 2 minutes, the hive returns to Stationary and fully re-evaluates.

→ Trade-off to Understand

While the hive is in any motion state (Starting, In Transit, or Stopping), HIVE doesn't fire breach alerts even if a tag is genuinely separated. You'll find out at the next stable rest. For most users this is fine — you can't usefully act on an alert while driving. Mid-journey detection is on the roadmap for future releases based on user need.

60-Day Lifetime

Every hive has a fixed 60-day operational lifetime from the moment of creation. This isn't a limitation — it's a hygiene mechanism that keeps your hive list focused on what's actually relevant. After expiry, the hive moves to a 60-day archive where you can still review history.

Recreating a Hive

The Recreate Hive button (visible on the hive detail page and in expiry notifications) opens a fresh wizard pre-filled with your current configuration and tags. One click and your tracking continues uninterrupted.

→ Why a 60-Day Cap?

Hives accumulate. Without a lifecycle, abandoned hives clutter the UI and consume tag slots indefinitely. The 60-day cap forces a periodic review — keep what matters, let the rest expire.

16

Delete & Recreate

Hive membership is locked at creation. To add or remove a tag from a hive, you must delete it and create a new one. This sounds restrictive but is actually faster than editing — the recreation flow pre-fills everything.

Two Kinds of Deletion

ActionResultReversible?
Delete Hive moves to archive · history retained for 60 days Yes, via Recreate
Permanently Delete All data removed immediately · no archive No

Both options are offered in the standard delete confirmation. Delete is the recoverable choice; Permanently Delete requires you to type the hive name to confirm.

The Recreate Flow

When you delete a hive then recreate, the wizard opens pre-populated with the deleted hive's configuration and tags. You can adjust anything — add or remove tags, change the threshold, switch mobility profile — and confirm. The new hive starts fresh with no inherited history.

→ One Tag, One Hive

A tag can only belong to one hive at a time. If you try to add a tag that's already in another hive, you'll see which hive it's in — delete that one first to free the tag.

17

Archive

The Archive view shows all your past hives — both deleted and expired — within their 60-day retention window. Each archived hive retains its full alert history, evaluation log, and configuration record.

What You Can Do in the Archive

  • Review past alerts, breaches, motion-state changes, and timelines
  • Recreate based on an archived hive's configuration
  • Permanently delete an archived hive to clear its data immediately

Auto-Purge

60 days after entering the archive, hives are automatically purged. You'll see a countdown clock on each archived card.

Troubleshooting

I'm getting fewer false breach alerts during car journeys — is something broken?

No — that's the v1.1 motion awareness working as intended. Previously HIVE would fire false breaches whenever you drove with your hive in the car. Those alerts are now correctly suppressed during transit. Real breaches at the destination still fire after you stop.

My hive shows "In Transit" but I'm not moving

This usually means several tags' position reports show movement that the system has interpreted as group motion. Most common causes:

  • Multiple tags reported new positions at roughly the same time, with reports coming from a moving phone (e.g. a passing vehicle)
  • Phone GPS noise was significant enough to look like motion

The state should self-correct within a few minutes once stable reports come in. If it persists, check whether your tags are actually stationary — sometimes things have moved without you realising.

My hive went into "In Transit" briefly when I was cycling or scooting

Cycling pace (15–20 km/h) crosses both the Slow profile motion threshold (10 km/h) and the Mobile profile threshold (15 km/h). If you carry your hive while cycling, transit detection will activate. This is expected and harmless — proximity evaluation resumes when you stop. Walking, however, should not trigger transit detection at either profile.

My hive shows "Partial Data" — is something wrong?

Usually no. Partial Data means too many tags in the hive haven't reported recently for HIVE to evaluate confidently. This is common in low-traffic areas where phones pass infrequently. Check the tag freshness pips: if most are amber or red, the hive is in a quiet area.

I'm getting too many alerts (when stationary)

Three things to check:

  • Is your distance threshold appropriate? Mobile-profile assets often need 200m+ to avoid noise
  • Is your mobility profile right? A stationary profile on actually-mobile assets will alert constantly
  • Are tag positions noisy? Low-quality phone GPS can produce 10–50m of jitter

I'm getting too few alerts

Either your threshold is too generous or your tags are reporting infrequently. Consider tightening the threshold and watch for a week. Also check that your hive isn't constantly stuck In Transit — if motion is being detected when it shouldn't be, alerts will be suppressed.

A tag I want to use says it's "in another hive"

Each tag can only belong to one hive at a time. The UI shows which hive it's currently in — delete that hive (or permanently delete it) to free the tag.

I changed the threshold but old alerts haven't updated

Configuration changes apply to future evaluations only — historical alerts remain as they were originally evaluated. This is intentional: it keeps your alert history meaningful and reproducible.

FAQ

How many tags can be in a hive?

Currently 2 to 5. Larger groups are on the roadmap.

Can a tag belong to multiple hives?

No. One tag, one hive at any given moment.

Is there a limit on how many hives I can have?

No, you can create as many as you like, subject only to having enough free tags for each.

Can I edit a hive's tags after creation?

No. Membership is locked at creation. To change membership, delete and recreate — the recreation flow makes this fast.

Can I extend a hive's lifetime past 60 days?

Not directly, but you can recreate the hive with the same configuration to start a new 60-day cycle. The recreation flow takes about 10 seconds.

Does HIVE work with my existing tags?

Yes. Any iBeacon or Eddystone tag registered in Pinplot can be added to a hive.

How quickly do alerts arrive?

Depends on how frequently your tags report. Urban areas typically see alerts within 3–8 minutes of a real breach. Rural areas can take 15–30 minutes. The system waits for confirmation to avoid false alarms.

Will I miss alerts while my hive is moving? v1.1

Standard breach alerts are paused during transit and resume when you stop. Any breach detected after the hive comes to rest will fire normally. Mid-journey detection is on the roadmap for future releases based on user need.

Can I turn off motion awareness? v1.1

Not directly — it's tied to your mobility profile. If you don't want motion detection, use the Stationary profile (which assumes any movement is unwanted and alerts you regardless). For other profiles, motion awareness is essential to avoid false positives.

What happens if my hive moves but only some tags report? v1.1

Motion detection requires several tags' positions to assess group coherence. If too few tags are reporting fresh positions, the system can't reliably determine motion state and stays in its current state until enough data is available.

What happens if a tag's battery dies?

It will go silent, transitioning through Stale to Lost over time. You'll receive a Lost Contact alert for that tag. Replace the tag and re-add it — note that you'll need to delete and recreate the hive to swap the tag.

Can I export hive history?

Export functionality is on the roadmap. For now, archived hives retain full history for 60 days, viewable in the UI.

What's the difference between Delete and Permanently Delete?

Delete moves the hive to a 60-day archive where you can still review history and recreate. Permanently Delete removes all data immediately and cannot be undone.
