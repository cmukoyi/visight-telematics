HIVE Tracking

Group your BLE tags into watched sets and get alerted the moment they drift apart. Built for travel kits, workshop tools, fleet assets, and anything else that should stay together.

01

Overview

HIVE is a proximity-tracking feature for groups of BLE tags. You select a set of 2 to 5 tags that belong together — a travel kit, a tool collection, a delivery load — and HIVE watches their positions. If any tag drifts further than your configured distance from the others, you get an alert.

HIVE works with any iBeacon or Eddystone BLE tags already registered in Pinplot. It uses the same crowdsourced location data your tags already report, no special hardware or anchor required. Tags report their position whenever a phone running a compatible app passes nearby, which means data quality is best in busy areas and naturally sparser in quiet ones.

→ At a Glance

Group 2–5 tags per hive · Configurable distance threshold · Per-hive mobility profile · 60-day operational lifetime · 60-day archive retention · Permanent delete option.

02

How It Works

Every time one of your tagged items reports a new position, HIVE re-evaluates the hive. It calculates the distance between every pair of tags in the group and finds the largest distance — the group diameter. If the diameter exceeds your threshold, the hive enters a breach state and you receive an alert.

The Alert State Machine

Alerts don't fire on the first sign of trouble — that would create noise from GPS jitter and brief edge cases. Instead, hives move through a deliberate sequence of states:

OK
→
PENDING
→
BREACHING
→
RESOLVING
→
OK
Figure 1. Alert state machine — hives need confirmation to enter breach and clear back to OK with hysteresis.

A suspected breach moves the hive to PENDING while waiting for a confirming report. Once confirmed, it transitions to BREACHING and the alert is sent. When the wandering tag rejoins, the hive doesn't immediately clear — it must drop comfortably below the threshold (hysteresis) before transitioning through RESOLVING back to OK.

Outlier Detection

When a breach fires, HIVE identifies which tag caused it by counting how many over-threshold pairs each tag participates in. The tag with the most breaching pairs is flagged as the outlier — usually the one that wandered off — and is highlighted in alerts and the detail view.

03

Quick Start

Getting your first hive running takes under a minute if you accept the suggested settings.

  1. From the sidebar, choose HIVE Tracking, then click + New Hive.
  2. Pick the tags you want to group (between 2 and 5). Tags already in another hive will be unavailable.
  3. Give the hive a name — something descriptive like Travel Kit or Workshop Tools.
  4. HIVE analyses your tags' recent movement and suggests a mobility profile and distance threshold. If the badge says High Confidence, click Use Suggested Settings and you're done.
  5. Otherwise, click Review Settings to walk through the 3-question wizard.

Your hive begins tracking immediately. There's a short 5–10 minute settling period where alerts are suppressed while HIVE establishes baseline state. After that, alerts fire normally.

→ Tip

If you've used HIVE before, the wizard remembers your last configuration. Recreating a hive with similar settings takes just a few clicks.

04

Key Concepts

Group Diameter

The largest distance between any two tags in your hive. If your hive has 5 tags, HIVE measures all 10 pairwise distances and reports the biggest one. The group is "together" when the diameter stays below your threshold.

Distance Threshold

The maximum group diameter you'll tolerate before an alert fires. Configurable per hive, and shown alongside the live diameter on every screen. Common values range from 50m (very close) to 1km (roughly together) depending on your use case.

Mobility Profile

A bundled preset that controls how HIVE interprets your tags' position data. Affects freshness windows, alert sensitivity, and confirmation requirements. Four options: Stationary, Slow, Mobile, and Fast.

Freshness Tier

Each tag is classified by how recently it reported. Tags fade through tiers as they go silent: FRESH RECENT STALE LOST. Only Fresh and Recent tags participate in alert evaluation.

Hive Lifecycle

Every hive has a 60-day operational life from the moment it's created. After that, it moves to a 60-day archive where you can review its history but it no longer tracks. After the archive period, data is purged unless you act.

05

Setup Wizard

The wizard is a 3-question flow designed to take seconds, not minutes. It pre-fills every answer based on your tags' recent activity, so most users can simply confirm and continue.

Question 1 — What are you tracking?

A descriptive name for the hive. Optional but recommended — alerts and history reference this name, so something concrete like Travel Kit beats Hive 4.

Question 2 — How much do these items move?

The mobility profile choice. HIVE pre-selects this based on how your tags have moved in the last few weeks, marked with a SUGGESTED badge. Four options to pick from — see Mobility Profiles for details.

Question 3 — How close should they stay?

The distance threshold. Choose from preset values (Very Close 50m, Close 100m, Nearby 250m, In the Same Area 500m, Roughly Together 1km) or enter a custom value. The default matches your selected mobility profile.

→ Express Path

If HIVE has high confidence in its suggestions (typically when your tags have at least 7 days of history), an express card appears at the top of the wizard. Click Use Suggested Settings to skip the questions entirely.

06

Mobility Profiles

Mobility profiles are the most important configuration choice. They tell HIVE what kind of asset you're tracking, which affects almost every aspect of how alerts are evaluated.

ProfileBest ForThreshold
Stationary Tools, equipment, instruments — assets that shouldn't normally move 50m
Slow Livestock, parked vehicles, mobility aids, luggage during a trip 100m
Mobile Personal belongings, shared equipment, items in daily use 200m
Fast Couriered packages, in-transit vehicles, actively-carried items 500m+

What Profiles Actually Change

Picking a profile sets several parameters at once. As mobility increases, freshness windows shorten (older positions become less trustworthy faster), confirmation requirements tighten (more reports needed before firing alerts), and noise filters widen (small position changes are ignored).

→ Picking the Wrong Profile

If your hive consistently produces too many false alerts or misses real ones, the profile is probably mismatched. HIVE will surface a gentle suggestion if it detects this. You can change the profile mid-life — see Delete & Recreate.

Hive List

The main HIVE screen shows all your active hives at a glance. Each card displays the hive name, current alert state, group diameter against threshold, tag freshness pips, the outlier (if any), and the lifecycle clock.

Status at a Glance

The coloured top border tells you the state instantly:

  • GREEN — All tags are together, hive is OK
  • AMBER — Partial data or freshness issues; hive can't fully evaluate
  • RED — Active breach; one or more tags have separated

08

Detail View

Clicking any hive card opens its detail view — the deepest workspace for understanding hive state and history.

Live Position Map

Shows each tag's most recent position relative to the others. The dashed circle marks your distance threshold. Connecting lines show pairwise distances — green lines for in-bounds pairs, dashed red lines for over-threshold pairs leading to the outlier.

Diameter Readout

A live numeric display of the current group diameter, shown alongside a horizontal bar chart with the threshold marker. When the bar crosses the marker, you're in breach — and the size of the overshoot tells you how serious the separation is.

Tag Table

A row for each tag showing ID, name, last-seen time, distance from group centre, and role (core or outlier). Sortable on any column. Stale or lost tags are dimmed.

Side Panels

The right column groups three panels:

  • Active Alert — Details of the current breach (if any), with cause and confirmation count
  • Configuration — Current mobility profile, threshold, freshness windows, and other parameters
  • Lifecycle — Days remaining on the 60-day clock, with a quick Recreate Hive button
  • Recent Activity — Timeline of evaluations, state changes, and alerts over the last 24 hours
09

Alerts & Breaches

HIVE issues different alert types depending on data quality and what changed. Knowing the difference helps you respond appropriately.

Alert TypeMeaningAction
Confirmed Breach Multiple fresh reports show the hive is split Verify and act — this is the most reliable signal
Suspected Breach One fresh report shows a breach, awaiting confirmation Watch for escalation; may auto-resolve
Lost Contact A tag has gone silent beyond the lost threshold Check tag battery and physical condition
Partially Observable Too many tags stale to evaluate confidently Often resolves on its own as data flows in
Breach Cleared The hive returned to OK state Informational — confirms the issue is resolved

Why Alerts May Take Time

Because tag positions arrive from crowdsourced sources, alert latency depends on how often phones pass nearby. In urban areas, breaches typically confirm within 3–8 minutes. In rural or low-traffic areas, it can take 15–30 minutes or longer. This isn't a system delay — it's the time required to gather enough evidence to be confident.

→ Honest About Uncertainty

HIVE will surface "data quality degraded" rather than fire low-confidence alerts. If you see a Partial Data warning, it means the hive can't confidently say what's happening — usually because tags have gone quiet, not because something's wrong.

60-Day Lifetime

Every hive has a fixed 60-day operational lifetime from the moment of creation. This isn't a limitation — it's a hygiene mechanism that keeps your hive list focused on what's actually relevant. After expiry, the hive moves to a 60-day archive where you can still review history.

Notifications as Expiry Approaches

You'll receive notifications at three points:

  • 7 days before expiry — first warning with one-tap recreate option
  • 2 days before expiry — second warning
  • At expiry — final notification with one-tap recreate option

Recreating a Hive

The Recreate Hive button (visible on the hive detail page and in expiry notifications) opens a fresh wizard pre-filled with your current configuration and tags. One click and your tracking continues uninterrupted.

→ Why a 60-Day Cap?

Hives accumulate. Without a lifecycle, abandoned hives clutter the UI and consume tag slots indefinitely. The 60-day cap forces a periodic review — keep what matters, let the rest expire.

12

Delete & Recreate

Hive membership is locked at creation. To add or remove a tag from a hive, you must delete it and create a new one. This sounds restrictive but is actually faster than editing — the recreation flow pre-fills everything.

Two Kinds of Deletion

ActionResultReversible?
Delete Hive moves to archive · history retained for 60 days Yes, via Recreate
Permanently Delete All data removed immediately · no archive No

Both options are offered in the standard delete confirmation. Delete is the recoverable choice; Permanently Delete requires you to type the hive name to confirm.

The Recreate Flow

When you delete a hive then recreate, the wizard opens pre-populated with the deleted hive's configuration and tags. You can adjust anything — add or remove tags, change the threshold, switch mobility profile — and confirm. The new hive starts fresh with no inherited history (the old hive's history remains in the archive).

→ One Tag, One Hive

A tag can only belong to one hive at a time. If you try to add a tag that's already in another hive, you'll see which hive it's in — delete that one first to free the tag.

13

Archive

The Archive view shows all your past hives — both deleted and expired — within their 60-day retention window. Each archived hive retains its full alert history, evaluation log, and configuration record.

What You Can Do in the Archive

  • Review past alerts, breaches, and timelines
  • Recreate based on an archived hive's configuration
  • Permanently delete an archived hive to clear its data immediately

Auto-Purge

60 days after entering the archive, hives are automatically purged. You'll see a countdown clock on each archived card — when it reaches zero, the hive's data is permanently removed and cannot be recovered.

Troubleshooting

My hive shows "Partial Data" — is something wrong?

Usually no. Partial Data means too many tags in the hive haven't reported recently for HIVE to evaluate confidently. This is common in low-traffic areas where phones pass infrequently. Check the tag freshness pips: if most are amber or red, the hive is in a quiet area. The status will resolve as new reports arrive.

I'm getting too many alerts

Three things to check:

  • Is your distance threshold appropriate? Mobile-profile assets often need 200m+ to avoid noise
  • Is your mobility profile right? A stationary profile on actually-mobile assets will alert constantly
  • Are tag positions noisy? Low-quality phone GPS can produce 10–50m of jitter

Adjust the threshold or change the mobility profile. Changes take effect going forward only — past alerts aren't recalculated.

I'm getting too few alerts

Either your threshold is too generous or your tags are reporting infrequently. Consider tightening the threshold by 25–50% and watch for a week. If alerts still don't fire when you expect them to, check whether your tags are reporting at all by reviewing their freshness.

A tag I want to use says it's "in another hive"

Each tag can only belong to one hive at a time. The UI shows which hive it's currently in — delete that hive (or permanently delete it) to free the tag.

The hive went into breach but my items are clearly together

Usually a single noisy GPS report. The breach should auto-clear within the next few evaluation cycles once the position settles. If it persists, check the outlier tag — its phone-source GPS may be reporting a wildly inaccurate position.

I changed the threshold but old alerts haven't updated

Configuration changes apply to future evaluations only — historical alerts remain as they were originally evaluated. This is intentional: it keeps your alert history meaningful and reproducible.

FAQ

How many tags can be in a hive?

Currently 2 to 5. Larger groups are on the roadmap.

Can a tag belong to multiple hives?

No. One tag, one hive at any given moment.

Is there a limit on how many hives I can have?

No, you can create as many as you like, subject only to having enough free tags for each.

Can I edit a hive's tags after creation?

No. Membership is locked at creation. To change membership, delete and recreate — the recreation flow makes this fast.

Can I extend a hive's lifetime past 60 days?

Not directly, but you can recreate the hive with the same configuration to start a new 60-day cycle. The recreation flow takes about 10 seconds.

Does HIVE work with my existing tags?

Yes. Any iBeacon or Eddystone tag registered in Pinplot can be added to a hive. You don't need special hardware.

How quickly do alerts arrive?

Depends on how frequently your tags report. Urban areas typically see alerts within 3–8 minutes of a real breach. Rural areas can take 15–30 minutes. The system waits for confirmation to avoid false alarms.

What happens if a tag's battery dies?

It will go silent, transitioning through Stale to Lost over time. You'll receive a Lost Contact alert for that tag. Replace the tag and re-add it — note that you'll need to delete and recreate the hive to swap the tag.

Can I export hive history?

Export functionality is on the roadmap. For now, archived hives retain full history for 60 days, viewable in the UI.

What's the difference between Delete and Permanently Delete?

Delete moves the hive to a 60-day archive where you can still review history and recreate. Permanently Delete removes all data immediately and cannot be undone.
